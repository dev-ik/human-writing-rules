# Entertainment Topic Foundation

## Classify the entertainment task

Identify whether the article is:

- news or release coverage;
- review;
- critical analysis;
- interview or profile;
- recommendation or guide;
- industry or reception analysis;
- retrospective.

Name the editorial position. A review requires legitimate access to the work or authorized author material. News coverage cannot silently inherit the distributor's promotional position.

## Work identity record

Before drafting, record when applicable:

- exact title, medium, edition, cut, translation, episode, season, platform, and region;
- release, screening, embargo, and availability dates;
- credited creators and the source of credits;
- award, sales, chart, or reception status and cutoff date;
- author's legitimate viewing, reading, listening, or playing basis;
- spoiler boundary;
- quotation and asset rights;
- evaluation criteria.

If edition, access, spoiler policy, or author experience materially affects the article and remains unknown, block or narrow the artifact.

## Evidence

- Separate facts about the work, creator statements, marketing claims, audience reception, critical interpretation, and the author's opinion.
- Verify title, edition or cut, release date, credits, region, availability, and award status when material.
- Do not invent consensus from a few reviews or social posts.
- Mark spoilers at the level expected by the audience and platform.
- Quote copyrighted dialogue, lyrics, scripts, or prose only when necessary and permitted; prefer concise quotation and analysis.

### Evidence classes

Keep these classes visible in notes and prose:

- verified identity, credit, date, edition, and availability;
- creator, performer, publisher, or distributor statement;
- marketing description or publicity claim;
- concrete observation from authorized access to the work;
- interpretation of that observation;
- declared author opinion;
- sampled audience response;
- documented critical consensus.

A few selected reviews or social posts do not establish audience or critical consensus. A creator statement may support intended meaning, but it does not settle the article's evaluation.

## Writing

- State the evaluation criteria behind praise or criticism.
- Use concrete observations about craft instead of generic superlatives.
- Preserve disagreement and genre expectations.
- Do not turn publicity copy into editorial fact.
- Avoid psychoanalyzing creators or performers without attributable evidence.

For evaluative writing, show the reasoning path:

> concrete observation → interpretation → criterion → bounded judgment

Choose criteria relevant to the form, such as structure, pacing, performance, mechanics, visual language, sound, genre use, translation, accessibility, or the relationship between form and theme.

Use first person only when the author actually supplied the experience. Do not fabricate viewing conditions, emotional reactions, memories, interviews, or behind-the-scenes knowledge.

Place spoiler warnings before the affected detail. A generic warning at the end does not protect the reader.

## Visuals

Use licensed promotional assets, owned materials, public-domain works, or original editorial illustrations. Do not imitate a living artist's signature style or imply that generated art is official. Avoid unlicensed logos, characters, likenesses, and scene reproductions.

Record the source, license or permission, credit, crop restrictions, territory, and disclosure for every selected asset.

An original generated illustration may express a broad editorial theme only when it avoids protected characters, recognizable performer likenesses, logos, key scene reconstruction, and false official affiliation. When rights or identity cannot be resolved, use no visual.

## Entertainment completion checks

- [ ] The exact work, edition, region, and availability are unambiguous where material.
- [ ] Author access and first-person perspective are legitimate.
- [ ] Fact, creator statement, marketing, observation, interpretation, opinion, and reception remain distinct.
- [ ] Praise and criticism use declared criteria and concrete observations.
- [ ] Spoiler boundaries appear before revealing details.
- [ ] Consensus and popularity claims have adequate evidence and a cutoff date.
- [ ] Quotations and visual assets respect copyright, likeness, trademark, credit, and disclosure requirements.
