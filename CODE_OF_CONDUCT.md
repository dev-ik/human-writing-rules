# Code of Conduct

Human Writing Rules is an evidence-oriented project whose rules may cross
languages, cultures, platforms, and professional domains. Contributors must
make disagreement safe, specific, and useful.

## Expected behavior

- Critique claims, contracts, examples, and evidence—not contributors.
- State the scope and limits of cultural or platform conventions.
- Ask for clarification before assigning motive.
- Protect private sources, personal data, credentials, and unpublished reports.
- Accept correction and repair harm when a contribution has an unintended
  effect.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, threats, stalking, discrimination, or sexualized attention.
- Personal attacks, deliberate humiliation, or sustained disruption.
- Publishing another person's private information without permission.
- Fabricating evidence, impersonating a contributor, or manipulating review.
- Using project spaces to promote abuse, evasion, fraud, or deceptive
  publication practices.

## Scope

This policy applies in repository issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions,
release coordination, and other spaces where a participant represents the
project.

## Reporting and enforcement

For a non-sensitive concern, ask the maintainer to moderate it in the relevant
issue or pull request. For a report that contains private information or could
expose a participant to retaliation, use GitHub private vulnerability reporting
for `dev-ik/human-writing-rules`, label the report as a conduct concern, and do
not include the details in a public issue.

The maintainer will review available evidence, avoid unnecessary disclosure,
give affected participants a reasonable opportunity to respond, and choose a
proportionate action. Actions may include a private warning, content removal,
temporary participation limits, or a permanent ban. A maintainer with a direct
conflict should seek a neutral reviewer when one is available.

Good-faith reporting is protected. Knowingly false or retaliatory reports are
themselves a violation.
