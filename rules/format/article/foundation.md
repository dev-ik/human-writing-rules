# Article Format Foundation

Use this module for long-form material that needs sustained explanation, multiple supported claims, or a navigable structure.

## Required framing

Before research or drafting, establish:

- the subject and central reader question;
- audience knowledge, needs, and likely misconceptions;
- intended outcome: explain, analyze, report, teach, review, argue, or narrate;
- topic and risk level;
- author perspective and legitimate basis for that perspective;
- target platform, approximate length, deadline, and freshness cutoff;
- supplied and required sources;
- visual mode and any asset constraints.

Clarify a missing item when it could change the thesis, evidence threshold, voice, or structure. Otherwise record a conservative inference.

## Article control record

Keep one internal record current throughout the run:

- exact subject and central reader question;
- reader promise and working thesis;
- primary article job and any necessary secondary job;
- audience knowledge, needs, and likely misconceptions;
- included, excluded, and unresolved questions;
- author perspective and its legitimate basis;
- topic, risk, platform, language, length range, deadline, and freshness cutoff;
- required source roles and supplied sources;
- visual mode, asset limit, and rights or accessibility constraints;
- current gate, artifact revision, and blockers.

If research changes the thesis or subject boundary, update this record and invalidate the affected outline, draft, media decision, and review.

## Define the reader promise

Write one internal sentence:

> After reading, the audience will understand **X**, know **Y**, and be able to **Z**.

Every major section must contribute to that promise. Remove background that is merely adjacent to the subject.

## Build the evidence plan

1. Break the central question into material claims.
2. Mark which claims need primary, current, or specialist sources.
3. Collect counterevidence, limitations, and unresolved questions.
4. Record dates, versions, jurisdictions, populations, and definitions that bound each claim.
5. Separate the author's interpretation from source claims.

Do not begin with a desired conclusion and search only for confirming material.

For every material source, preserve its role, identity, author or issuing body, publication and retrieval dates, version or scope, relevant support, limitations, and access or rights restrictions.

Stop research and return a gap when the requested conclusion depends on a missing primary record, incompatible versions, unresolved identity, insufficient freshness, or evidence that cannot establish the claim.

## Choose a structure

Use the smallest structure that matches the reader's path. Common options include:

- **Explainer:** question → essential context → mechanism → implications → limits.
- **Analysis:** thesis → evidence → alternatives or objections → bounded conclusion.
- **Reported article:** verified development → context → attributed perspectives → unknowns → next signals.
- **Tutorial:** result → prerequisites → steps → verification → failure modes.
- **Review:** evaluation criteria → evidence → strengths and limits → suitable audience → verdict.
- **Opinion:** explicit position → reasons → strongest objection → response → conclusion.
- **Narrative:** real event → relevant detail → tension or change → honest meaning without a forced universal lesson.

These are starting patterns, not mandatory templates. Combine or omit sections when the reader benefits.

Name one primary article job. When jobs are combined, record the reader need served by each one. A tutorial section does not turn vendor claims elsewhere in the article into reproduced behavior. A personal opening does not authorize first-person claims that are absent from author material.

## Outline with claims

For every planned section, record:

- its reader question;
- its main claim;
- supporting evidence;
- necessary attribution or qualification;
- transition to the next section;
- possible visual role.

A heading should reveal useful structure. Do not create headings merely to break up text.

Order sections by information dependency, not by the order in which sources were collected. Put necessary background where the reader first needs it. Place material limitations beside the affected claim rather than reserving all caveats for the end.

## Media decision

For `auto`, select a visual only when it has a specific explanatory, orienting, or editorial benefit. Record `none` when a table, code sample, quotation, or the article itself communicates the point more accurately.

For each selected asset, define:

- purpose, placement, and reader benefit;
- verified content it may depict and implications it must avoid;
- asset type and whether generation is appropriate;
- composition, hierarchy, dimensions, aspect ratio, and crop;
- caption, alt text, credit, provenance, disclosure, and rights;
- acceptance criteria and fallback.

Use a verified asset or manually constructed graphic when exact appearance, data, anatomy, code, interface state, map, quotation, or historical detail matters.

## Draft

- Lead with the subject, reader problem, or verified development.
- Establish relevance without theatrical suspense.
- Put evidence near the claim it supports.
- Define necessary terms at first useful mention.
- Use examples to clarify a mechanism, not to substitute for evidence.
- Preserve proportion: limitations should be visible where they affect interpretation.
- Use quotes only when the exact wording or speaker matters.
- Keep paragraphs as long as the thought requires.

Write the body before optimizing the title and lead. A strong title accurately compresses the finished article; it does not promise a more dramatic conclusion than the evidence supports.

## Source presentation

Choose a source interface appropriate to the platform: inline links, explicit attribution, footnotes, endnotes, a source list, or a separate methodology note.

The reader should be able to identify the authority and uncertainty behind material claims without seeing the private claim ledger. Preserve exact attribution where a statement is disputed, interpretive, preliminary, or dependent on the speaker's authority.

## Article elements

Use only elements that serve the publication:

- title;
- deck or standfirst;
- byline and date;
- lead;
- body sections;
- callouts, tables, code, or pull quotes;
- conclusion;
- source list or inline citations;
- author note or disclosure;
- visual assets with captions and alt text;
- platform metadata.

Do not force a CTA, FAQ, summary box, or numbered list.

## Edit in passes

1. **Truth:** claims, attribution, dates, uncertainty, and source fit.
2. **Argument:** thesis, section order, counterevidence, and conclusion.
3. **Reader:** assumed knowledge, definitions, examples, and cognitive load.
4. **Language:** native phrasing, precision, rhythm, and register.
5. **Compression:** repetition, empty setup, generic significance, and decorative structure.
6. **Platform:** length, markup, links, metadata, and media placement.
7. **Visual alignment:** the asset communicates the intended idea without adding unsupported content.

## Completion gate

An article is publication-ready only when:

- the title and lead match the article actually written;
- every material claim is supported, attributed, qualified, or removed;
- fact, source claim, inference, and opinion remain distinguishable;
- the central reader question is answered within the available evidence;
- meaningful limitations and conflicts are present;
- the conclusion does not overreach the body;
- required disclosures and source notes are complete;
- every selected visual has passed factual, accessibility, rights, provenance, and platform review;
- blocker and major review findings are resolved.

If the evidence cannot support the intended article, narrow the promise or return a research gap instead of padding the draft.

## Blocked result

When the completion gate cannot pass, return:

- the blocked stage;
- affected claim, section, or asset;
- missing decision or evidence;
- why it matters;
- smallest action that could unblock the article;
- safe partial artifacts, if any.

Do not return an incomplete draft under a publication-ready label.
