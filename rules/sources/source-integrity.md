# Source Integrity

## Source record

For every source used materially, preserve:

- title or identity;
- author, publisher, or responsible organization;
- publication and update date when available;
- retrieval date for mutable sources;
- stable locator;
- source type and relationship to the claim;
- relevant version, jurisdiction, population, or timeframe;
- conflicts, sponsorship, or access limitations when material.

## Claim ledger

Classify each material statement as:

- verified fact;
- attributed source claim;
- opinion;
- inference;
- assumption;
- unknown.

Map material facts and source claims to supporting sources. Record conflicts instead of silently choosing the most convenient source.

## Selection

- Prefer primary sources for specifications, laws, research, product behavior, filings, and official events.
- Use independent secondary sources for context, criticism, verification, and synthesis.
- Do not infer current state from an undated page.
- Do not turn absence of evidence into evidence of absence.
- Do not use a search result snippet as the source.
- Match source freshness and authority to the risk of the claim.

## Use

- Attribute claims at the level the evidence supports.
- Preserve uncertainty when sources conflict.
- Paraphrase rather than imitate source voice.
- Quote only when exact wording matters and the quotation is permitted.
- Do not extend a source beyond its population, timeframe, jurisdiction, version, or measured outcome.
- Recalculate derived values and preserve units.

The same rules apply to captions, alt text, chart labels, diagrams, visual prompts, and generated assets.
