# Social Post Format Foundation

Use this module for a short platform-native artifact built around one dominant idea or action.

## Frame

Define the audience, single communication goal, topic, source basis, author perspective, platform, and visual mode. Decide whether the post stands alone or points to a longer artifact.

If the subject needs several qualifications or a long evidence chain, publish an article and adapt a post from it rather than compressing away the limits.

## Post control record

Keep one internal record with:

- exact subject and one communication goal;
- reader outcome and primary post job;
- audience and expected feed context;
- standalone, gateway, response, or ordered-series form;
- topic, risk, platform, language, and current constraint cutoff;
- author perspective and its legitimate basis;
- primary claim, essential support, important limitation, and unknowns;
- source and link roles;
- visual mode, asset count, and media rights or accessibility constraints;
- real reader action, if one exists;
- current artifact revision, gate, and blockers.

Changing the subject, claim status, link destination, platform constraint, or asset invalidates affected copy, preview, metadata, and review.

## Claim budget

Use short length to reduce the number of claims, not their evidence threshold.

Plan:

- one primary claim, announcement, story, question, or action;
- only the facts needed to understand it;
- the most important qualification;
- optional context that may be removed safely;
- the next step, when one exists.

Every material claim must map to a source, authorized author material, or a declared opinion or inference. When identity, attribution, date, version, status, or limitation changes the meaning, keep it in the post rather than only behind a link.

## Context gate

Draft only when:

- subject, audience, goal, topic, platform, language, and perspective are known;
- the primary claim has adequate support;
- status, date, version, availability, and uncertainty are current where material;
- the post remains honest when forwarded, quoted, truncated, or separated from a link;
- platform constraints are known where they affect the artifact;
- visual mode and rights boundaries are explicit.

If the claim cannot fit without losing a material boundary, narrow it, create an ordered series, use an article, or return a blocked result.

## Design

- State the subject or relevance early.
- Keep one primary claim, story, announcement, or question.
- Use enough context for the claim to remain honest when separated from linked material.
- Put the most important limitation in the post, not only behind a link.
- Use line breaks for reading comfort, not artificial suspense.
- Add a CTA only when the reader has a real next step.
- Use links, hashtags, mentions, emojis, and threads proportionately.

Give each content unit a job: identify the subject, explain relevance, provide essential evidence, preserve a boundary, or offer a real next step. Do not force every job into every post.

Open with a verified development, concrete observation, reader problem, or direct subject. Do not manufacture suspense, universality, shock, personal confession, or an unexplained number.

End with the conclusion, source, next signal, real action, or genuine question that completes the post. Do not force a CTA or request for engagement.

## Media decision

For `auto`, choose media only when it explains, verifies, or usefully frames the post. Record `none` when the copy, link preview, table, code, or source asset already communicates the idea more accurately.

For every selected asset, define:

- purpose, placement, reader benefit, and relationship to the copy;
- verified content it may show and implications it must avoid;
- source or reason generation is appropriate;
- dimensions, aspect ratio, crop, safe area, and first-frame behavior;
- prompt or construction instructions;
- caption, alt text, credit, provenance, disclosure, and rights;
- acceptance criteria and fallback.

Treat crop and preview as part of the claim. Use a verified asset when exact appearance, data, quotation, person, event, work, product, or interface matters.

## Draft and compression

Draft the complete meaning before optimizing the opening, line breaks, CTA, tags, or media copy.

Compress in this order:

1. duplicate setup;
2. optional background;
3. decorative transition;
4. secondary example;
5. redundant metadata or CTA.

Do not remove identity, attribution, date, version, status, material limitation, disclosure, or rights context merely to fit a limit.

## Adaptation

When adapting an article:

1. choose one useful idea instead of summarizing every section;
2. preserve the article's evidence boundary;
3. rewrite natively for the platform;
4. link to the article when it supplies necessary depth;
5. choose a platform-appropriate visual crop or create a separate brief.

Do not paste the article lead and remove context until it fits.

Pin the final article revision and source notes before adapting. Review the post as a separate artifact because platform context, crop, link behavior, and the selected claim may differ from the article.

## Ordered series

Use a series only when the material cannot be narrowed safely to one post.

- Define one series promise and a function for each unit.
- Keep identity, attribution, and material qualification recoverable.
- Make order explicit.
- Review every unit alone and in sequence.
- Do not split an article at arbitrary length boundaries.

## Completion gate

- The post has one clear purpose.
- It remains accurate without relying on invisible context.
- Platform conventions do not override audience needs.
- The perspective is legitimate and any personal experience is real.
- The ending fits the purpose; engagement bait is absent.
- Visuals, links, tags, and disclosure are complete when applicable.

## Blocked result

When the gate cannot pass, return:

- affected claim, unit, link, or asset;
- missing evidence or decision;
- why the missing context is material;
- smallest action that could unblock publication;
- whether a narrower post or longer format is safe.

Do not return incomplete copy under a publication-ready label.
