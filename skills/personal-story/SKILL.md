# Personal Story Skill

Use only a real event, observation, feeling, and sequence supplied by the author.

## Workflow

1. Identify what the author directly remembers, what records support, and what remains uncertain.
2. Preserve the author's legitimate perspective and privacy boundaries.
3. Select details because they change meaning, not because they make the story appear human.
4. Keep reconstructed chronology or dialogue explicitly qualified.
5. Separate the author's interpretation from general claims.
6. Preserve ambiguity and mixed outcomes.

Do not fabricate dialogue, sensory detail, emotions, conflict, or resolution. Do not retrofit every story into a universal lesson.

Use a visual only when it comes from an approved real asset or is clearly a conceptual editorial illustration. Never generate a scene and present it as the author's memory.
