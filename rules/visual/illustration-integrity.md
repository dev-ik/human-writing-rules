# Visual and Illustration Integrity

Visuals are optional editorial artifacts. Generate one only when it has a defined reader benefit.

## Select a purpose

Use one primary purpose:

- **Editorial:** establish a truthful mood or conceptual frame.
- **Explanatory:** clarify a mechanism, comparison, sequence, or spatial relationship.
- **Cover:** identify the subject in feeds or article listings.
- **Data visualization:** present verified values and relationships.
- **Documentary:** show a real person, place, object, event, or interface.

Generated imagery may serve editorial, explanatory, or cover purposes. It must not be presented as documentary evidence. Data visualizations and technical diagrams require verified source data and manual label review.

If the purpose is only “the template has an image slot,” choose no visual.

## Visual gate

Before generation, record:

- intended placement and reader benefit;
- verified content the image may depict;
- content it must not imply;
- asset type and whether generation is appropriate;
- composition, orientation, dimensions, and aspect ratio;
- art direction and brand constraints;
- required labels, caption, alt text, credit, and disclosure;
- source, copyright, trademark, likeness, privacy, and cultural constraints;
- acceptance criteria.

Do not generate until this gate passes.

## Prompt construction

Build the prompt from the approved brief, not from decorative adjectives alone. Describe subject, action or relationship, setting, composition, visual hierarchy, medium, palette when relevant, aspect ratio, and prohibited implications.

Do not request imitation of a living artist's signature style. Do not use a real person's likeness, protected character, logo, or proprietary interface unless permission and context support it.

Avoid image text when possible. Generated labels, numbers, equations, code, maps, and interface elements require manual verification.

## Factual boundaries

- Do not invent experimental results, product features, historical scenes, data, anatomy, locations, scale, or causal relationships.
- Label conceptual, reconstructed, simulated, or generated visuals when the context could otherwise mislead.
- A caption must not upgrade an illustration into evidence.
- Use the original verified asset instead of generation when exact appearance is material.
- For sensitive events or vulnerable people, consider whether generation creates false documentation or unnecessary harm.

## Accessibility and handoff

Provide:

- descriptive filename;
- dimensions and format;
- concise alt text for the visual's communicative content;
- caption when context, source, or disclosure is needed;
- credit and rights note;
- generation prompt or provenance when requested;
- crop-safe guidance for target platforms.

Alt text should describe meaning, not every decorative detail. Do not repeat a nearby caption verbatim.

## Review

Compare the visual against the final text, evidence map, brief, and platform crop. Check factual consistency, misleading implications, legibility, contrast-sensitive choices, accessibility, rights, disclosure, and whether the visual still earns its place.

Revise or omit a failed asset. A publication-ready text does not become incomplete merely because an optional visual was rejected.
